# One ID formalization survey

This survey lists every rule in IATA's One ID Technical Implementation Guide, version 26.3, that falls in the scope of this project. Each rule is graded by the test below before any Lean is written for it. The grade decides how the rule enters the blueprint: a clear rule becomes an ordinary node, and a vague or silent one becomes a node marked not ready, with the point the business has to decide.

This repository is a proof of concept. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by IATA, and IATA owns the guide.

Surveyed on 2026-11-05 from the approved 26.3 document, read in full. An independent reviewer then checked every requirement word of the in-scope sections against the entries; the regrading and the four entries that review added are included here. The guide states no license, so each entry describes its rule in our own words, cites the section it comes from, and quotes at most a few words. Identifiers such as `bcbp_string` are used as names.

## Scope

In scope: the functional requirements and assumptions (§3), the photoid and boarding pass data models (§4.1), and remote and proximity presentation (§4.2). Annexes A and B are informative, so they are surveyed only where they constrain the normative sections. The guide interprets MUST, SHOULD and MAY as requirement words only when they are in capitals (§1.2), so a lower-case "must" in an annex or a heading is not counted as a requirement.

The guide builds on ISO, OpenID, W3C, IETF, ICAO and IATA specifications that this project has not read. An entry that depends on one names it in the blueprint under External, and `Rules/Core/Assumptions.lean` lists every such dependency with its citation. The model takes from an external specification only what the guide says about it.

## How rules were graded

Each entry has a kind:

* **Structure**: what a credential, a request or a response contains.
* **Obligation**: something a party must, should or may do.
* **Check**: something a party verifies before it accepts or answers.
* **Guarantee**: a property the rules together are meant to give.

Each entry also records what the guide says about it:

* **Clear**: the guide states the rule.
* **Vague**: the guide states the rule with an undefined term, or states it twice at different strengths, or in a sentence that can be read two ways.
* **Silent**: the guide names the situation and gives no rule for it, or puts it out of scope.

The level is the guide's own requirement word. An entry can be clear and still depend on an external specification: the guide clearly says which clause applies, and this project has not read the clause.

Several requirement words of the guide can share one entry when they state one rule, such as the parameters of a request. IDs never renumber: C-97 to C-100 were added after an independent review of the first survey, so they sit out of order in their areas. The Model column gives the requirement in Lean for an entry that is modelled, and "candidate" for one that is a blueprint node with no Lean yet.

## What the guide puts out of scope, and the guarantees it blocks

The guide puts the trust model, revocation and normative issuance out of scope (§1.4). Each is a silent entry. A guarantee is blocked by one when its proof would need a rule the entry does not give.

| Entry | What the guide says | Guarantees it blocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C-01 The trust model | The guide puts the trust model out of scope: how trust is anchored, delegated and verified between issuer, wallet and verifier. It still relies on lists of trusted issuers and trusted verifiers throughout §3.2 and §4.2. | G-01 (the wallet cannot say whom data is shared with unless verifiers are on a list it trusts), G-02 (a device key proves holder binding only if the issuer that signed it is trusted), G-03 (a boarding pass and a photoid come from different issuers, and matching them assumes both are trusted), G-04 (the validity a verifier checks is the one a trusted issuer signed) |
| C-02 Revocation | The guide puts revocation out of scope. Within its rules a credential stops being accepted when the validity its issuer signed ends, or when its issuer is no longer trusted. | G-04 (an expired credential is refused, a withdrawn one is not), G-03 (a boarding pass that was reissued to a rebooked passenger still matches the photoid), G-02 (a device key on a stolen phone stays bound) |
| C-03 Issuance to the wallet | The guide puts normative issuance out of scope and gives only an informative annex. It assumes credentials are securely issued (§3.2). The rules it does give that are fixed at issuance are the content of each credential (§4.1.1, §4.1.2) and the end of validity of a boarding pass. | G-02 (binding to the device key starts at issuance), G-03 (the subject of each credential is set at issuance) |
