# One ID formalization survey

This survey lists every rule in IATA's One ID Technical Implementation Guide, version 26.3, that falls in the scope of this project. Each rule is graded by the test below before any Lean is written for it. The grade decides how the rule enters the blueprint: a clear rule becomes an ordinary node, and a vague or silent one becomes a node marked not ready, with the point the business has to decide.

This repository is a proof of concept. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by IATA, and IATA owns the guide.

Surveyed on 2026-11-05 from the approved 26.3 document, read in full. An independent reviewer then checked every requirement word of the in-scope sections against the entries; the regrading and the four entries that review added are included here. The guide states no license, so each entry describes its rule in our own words, cites the section it comes from, and quotes at most a few words. Identifiers such as `bcbp_string` are used as names.

## Scope

In scope: the functional requirements and assumptions (§3), the photoid and boarding pass data models (§4.1), and remote and proximity presentation (§4.2). Annexes A and B are informative, so they are surveyed only where they constrain the normative sections. The guide interprets MUST, SHOULD and MAY as requirement words only when they are in capitals (§1.2), so a lower-case "must" in an annex or a heading is not counted as a requirement.

The guide builds on ISO, OpenID, W3C, IETF, ICAO and IATA specifications. An entry that depends on one names it in the blueprint under External, and `Rules/Core/Assumptions.lean` lists every such dependency with its citation and says whether this project has read the specification. The public ones were fetched and read after the first survey: OpenID4VP, the OpenID4VC high assurance profile, the W3C draft, ICAO Doc 9303 parts 10 and 11, IATA's public guide to the boarding pass bar code, and four RFCs. `docs/external-survey.md` records what each cited clause says and where it differs from the guide, and an entry here that states what one of them says names the document and the clause. The ISO/IEC documents and IATA Resolution 792 are sold and were not read. The model takes from those only what the guide says about them.

## How rules were graded

Each entry has a kind:

* **Structure**: what a credential, a request or a response contains.
* **Obligation**: something a party must, should or may do.
* **Check**: something a party verifies before it accepts or answers.
* **Guarantee**: a property the rules together are meant to give.

Each entry also records what the guide says about it:

* **Clear**: the guide states the rule.
* **Vague**: the guide states the rule with an undefined term, or states it twice at different strengths, or in a sentence that can be read two ways.
* **Silent**: the guide names the situation and gives no rule for it, or puts it out of scope.

The column "Answered by reading" names a clause of a specification the guide cites when reading that clause would settle the entry's open point, and this project has not read that specification. Such an entry needs a source, where the other vague and silent entries need a decision from the people who own the standard. Every clause still named there is in a document that is sold. When a clause is read, the column is emptied: the point is then answered, or it is a decision. C-72 went that way when OpenID4VP was read.

The level is the guide's own requirement word. An entry can be clear and still depend on an external specification: the guide clearly says which clause applies, whether or not this project has read the clause.

Several requirement words of the guide can share one entry when they state one rule, such as the parameters of a request. IDs never renumber: C-97 to C-100 were added after an independent review of the first survey, C-101 to C-103 when lookups were separated from decisions, C-104 when the public specifications were read, and C-105 to C-107 when the presentation flows were written in Lean, so they sit out of order in their areas. The Model column gives the requirement in Lean for an entry that is modelled, and "candidate" for one that is a blueprint node with no Lean yet.
